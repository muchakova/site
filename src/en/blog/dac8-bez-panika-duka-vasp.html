The amendments to the Tax and Social Security Procedure Code (TSSPC) that brought DAC8 into Bulgarian law were only promulgated on 15 September 2026 (State Gazette No. 85), but they apply from 1 January 2026. In practice this means three things. 2026 is already a reporting year. The first report to the National Revenue Agency (NRA) is due by 30 June 2027. And the two most important client due diligence deadlines expire in December 2026.

In this article I briefly look at what three groups of companies need to do: licensed crypto-asset service providers (CASPs), companies that are in the process of obtaining a licence, and former VASPs that have ceased operations. The answers for the three groups differ more than they seem to at first glance.

DAC8 in brief

DAC8 (Directive (EU) 2023/2226) is the European implementation of the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF). In Bulgaria it was introduced through the new Section IIIb in Chapter Sixteen of the TSSPC (Articles 142yu–142ya10). The logic is familiar from the banking sector, where the Common Reporting Standard (CRS) has applied for years. Now the same applies to crypto-assets.

  • Who reports. Anyone who, as a business, carries out exchange transactions for or on behalf of clients. This covers both CASPs licensed under MiCA and so-called “crypto-asset operators” that have no licence. Staking and lending also count as services for DAC8 purposes.
  • On whom. On clients who are residents of any EU Member State, including Bulgaria, as well as of partner jurisdictions outside the EU.
  • What. Not income and not profit, but annual aggregated data for each client and each type of crypto-asset: gross amounts, fair market value, number of units and number of transactions. Purchases and sales against fiat currency, exchanges between crypto-assets, payments for goods and services above USD 50,000 and transfers, including withdrawals to the clients’ own wallets, are reported separately.
  • How. Every client submits a declaration of their tax residence (self-certification). The provider checks its reasonableness against the data collected during identification under the Measures Against Money Laundering Act.
  • When. The report to the NRA is due by 30 June of the following year. The NRA exchanges the data with the other Member States by 30 September.

Why it is urgent. The data that will be reported in 2027 is being generated now. If the client declarations are not collected by December, there is no way to complete the report correctly in June 2027.

1. Licensed CASP

A company licensed by the FSC is a “reporting crypto-asset service provider” under Article 142ya(2)(1) TSSPC. It does not register separately with the NRA: the FSC provides the agency with a list of licensed providers by 31 January of the following year. There are seven tasks.

  1. Define the scope. Which services involve exchange transactions, which crypto-assets are reportable and which clients are excluded persons (companies whose shares are traded on a regulated market, governmental entities, central banks, financial institutions, etc.).
  2. Complete both self-certification campaigns. Clients onboarded in 2026 must be checked by 15.12.2026, and pre-existing clients by 01.01.2027. From then on, the declaration is obtained when every new account is opened, before the first transaction.
  3. Switch on the “lock”. A client who has not provided a declaration 60 days after the request and after at least two written reminders may not carry out transactions (Article 142ya8(1)). For clients onboarded in 2026 the rule is even stricter: no completed due diligence, no transactions (§ 29 of the Transitional Provisions). Every transaction allowed costs a penalty of €150 per client.
  4. Prepare the data. Distinguish exchanges from transfers and classify transfers by type. Flag withdrawals to the clients’ own (non-custodial) wallets. Choose one price source and one fair value method and apply them consistently. Ideally, this is the same source you use for accounting and for capital adequacy.
  5. Inform your clients. For exchange purposes the CASP is a data controller. It must notify individuals in advance which data will be passed to the NRA (Article 143p1(4) TSSPC). The easiest way is to include the notice in the declaration request.
  6. File the report by 30 June. If there is nothing to report, a notice is filed instead (Article 142ya2(8)). Failing to file it is also penalised.
  7. Keep the audit trail. Declarations, reminders, methodologies and reports are kept for 10 years.

External providers. You can outsource the checks to a KYC platform or the reports to software, but the responsibility remains yours (Article 142ya6(5) TSSPC). The contract must cover the due diligence standard, deadlines, audit rights and document retention.

If the licence was granted during 2026 and the company had operated under the transitional regime until then, a particular question arises. The law does not expressly address how a change of status within a single reporting year is treated: from crypto-asset operator to licensed provider. The company will appear on the FSC list for 2026, but some of the transactions took place before the licence. I recommend requesting a written opinion from the NRA on this point.

2. CASP in the process of licensing

On 1 July 2026 the MiCA transitional period ended across the EU. Since then, crypto-asset services may only be provided with a licence, and ESMA expects unlicensed providers to wind down their activities in an orderly manner. A company still waiting for an FSC decision therefore does not provide services today. Its DAC8 obligations depend on whether it operated during 2026.

Scenario A: you operated under the transitional regime in 2026

Registration in the register under § 5(3) of the Transitional and Final Provisions of the Markets in Crypto-Assets Act is not a MiCA licence: the FSC says so explicitly as well. For the period up to 30.06.2026 the company is therefore a crypto-asset operator under Article 142ya(2)(2) TSSPC. What follows:

  • Register with the NRA as an operator (Article 142ya1). The statutory deadline coincides with the report deadline, i.e. 30.06.2027, but do not put it off. The penalty for operating without registration is €5,000–10,000. Changes to the registration data must be notified within 14 days.
  • Check the clients from the period of activity within the same deadlines: by 15.12.2026 for those onboarded in 2026 and by 01.01.2027 for pre-existing ones.
  • Report the transactions from 1 January to 30 June 2026 by 30.06.2027. Also include transfers after 30 June if you returned assets to clients.
  • Prepare for the licence. The DAC8 step must be built into the client onboarding procedures you present to the FSC. A 2026 client without completed due diligence cannot trade once activity resumes.
  • After the licence you move to the licensed provider regime and notify the NRA that your operator registration should be deleted (Article 142ya7(5)(3)).

Scenario B: a new applicant that has not yet provided services

With no transactions there is nothing to report, so such a company does not register as an operator. DAC8 must nevertheless be part of the business model from day one:

  • client identification including tax residence and tax identification number (TIN);
  • the technical ability to block an account;
  • a transaction register with all the fields required for the report;
  • a fair value methodology.

The first report is due by 30 June of the year following the year of the licence.

If activity continues without a licence. A company that keeps providing services without a licence after 1 July 2026 is in breach of MiCA. DAC8 does not legalise that activity, but it does not release the company from its obligations either: it remains a crypto-asset operator and must report.

3. A VASP that has ceased operations entirely

Here the answer depends on the date operations stopped.

  • Operations stopped before 01.01.2026 and in 2026 there was not a single transaction or transfer for clients, including the return of assets. DAC8 creates no obligations.
  • Operations stopped during 2026, typically on 30.06.2026. The company is an operator for the part of the year it worked. Ceasing operations does not cancel the obligations, because the law applies from 1 January.

In the second case the path is as follows:

  1. Registration with the NRA as an operator under Article 142ya1.
  2. Self-certification from former clients within the 15.12.2026 and 01.01.2027 deadlines. A practical approach is to pre-fill the declaration with data from the KYC file, so that the client only has to confirm their residence and tax number electronically. Every request and reminder is recorded.
  3. Clients who do not respond. The law does not expressly cover the case where the relationship has ended and the client stays silent. Blocking does not help, because there are no transactions anyway. In my view, a reasonable approach is:
    • a documented request and at least two written reminders;
    • for Bulgarian clients, the personal number (EGN/LNCh) on file serves as the tax number;
    • without a declaration, residence is determined from the most reliable KYC data, and the approach is described in an internal procedure;
    • a written enquiry to the NRA.
    This is an interpretation, not established practice.
  4. Report by 30.06.2027 for transactions up to the date operations ceased.
  5. Notice to individuals under Article 143p1(4) TSSPC before the report is filed.
  6. Exit from the register. After the report, the company notifies the NRA that it no longer has reportable users. Its registration is deleted from the European Commission’s central register (Article 142ya7(5)(1)).
  7. A 10-year archive. If the company is to be liquidated, decide now who will keep the records and how.

The maths is simple. The penalty for failing to carry out due diligence is €150 per client (Article 278i(4) TSSPC). With 2,000 former clients that is €300,000, far more than the cost of a well-organised email campaign.

Four common misconceptions

  • “Only foreigners are reported.” No. Bulgarian residents are reported too (§ 1a(74) of the Supplementary Provisions of the TSSPC). The NRA simply does not send their data abroad but receives it directly.
  • “The client’s income is reported.” No. Turnover is reported. Clients still declare their income themselves in their annual tax return, and the NRA will compare the two.
  • “We stopped, so we have no obligations.” Not if you operated in 2026. The law applies from 1 January.
  • “A stablecoin is a token like any other.” Not necessarily. Electronic money is excluded from reportable crypto-assets and is treated as fiat currency (§ 1a(61), (62) and (66) of the Supplementary Provisions of the TSSPC).
    • USDT does not meet the condition of redemption at par value by virtue of regulatory requirements, so it is reported like any other crypto-asset.
    • USDC and EURC (EMTs under MiCA). A literal reading leads to the conclusion that they meet the five criteria for electronic money. In that case, buying ETH with USDC is a purchase against fiat currency. The conclusion is not beyond dispute, however, because Recital 14 of DAC8 expressly includes e-money tokens in the concept of a “crypto-asset”.
    The company’s position must therefore be documented and applied consistently.

Five things for this week

  1. Determine your status: licensed provider, operator or out of scope.
  2. Segment your clients by the two deadlines: onboarded in 2026 and pre-existing as at 31.12.2025.
  3. Send out the declaration requests together with the personal data notice.
  4. Appoint a responsible person and, if you use an external provider, sign a contract with them.
  5. Watch the NRA website for the order on the report format, the procedure for registering operators and the list of partner jurisdictions. As at the date of this article I have not found these documents published.
